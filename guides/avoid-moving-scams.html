How to avoid moving scams

The most expensive moving mistakes happen before the truck arrives: hiring an unregistered company, accepting a too-good phone quote, or signing a blank form. The Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration (FMCSA) regulates interstate movers and brokers and publishes nine core tips.

FMCSA's 9 tips, in plain English

  1. Know your rights. Your mover must give you the booklet Your Rights and Responsibilities When You Move.
  2. Learn the red flags (below).
  3. Verify registration. Interstate movers and brokers need a USDOT number and FMCSA registration — search it on FMCSA's mover lookup.
  4. Get the documents: a written Estimate, the Bill of Lading and an Inventory.
  5. Brokers must provide a written estimate too — and tell you they are a broker, not the mover.
  6. Never sign blank or incomplete documents.
  7. Choose your liability coverage. Basic “released value” pays only 60¢ per pound per item; Full Value Protection covers repair, replacement or cash value.
  8. Supervise loading and unloading, or appoint someone.
  9. Use the planning tools — the Ready to Move brochure and a checklist like ours.

Red flags

  • A quote by phone or email with no inventory survey (in person or video).
  • A large cash deposit demanded before the move.
  • No USDOT number on the website or truck; a rented, unmarked truck on moving day.
  • The company name changes, or the “mover” turns out to be a broker who never said so.
  • No local address or insurance information; poor or no reviews.
  • Pressure to sign an incomplete contract — or a sudden higher price once your goods are loaded (“hostage load”).

If something goes wrong

File a complaint with FMCSA's National Consumer Complaint Database (1-800-832-5660), keep every document and photo, and contact your state attorney general. Check the BBB Scam Tracker before booking.

Sources: FMCSA “Protect Your Move — Tips for Success”; Opendoor moving cost guide.
